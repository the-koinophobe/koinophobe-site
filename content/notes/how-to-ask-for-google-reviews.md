Reviews are one of the strongest things you control in local search. They help you show up in the map, help people choose you once you do, and they're quoted by AI assistants when they recommend local businesses. Most home service companies have far fewer than they could, simply because they don't ask, or ask at the wrong time.

## Ask at the right moment

The best time is right when the job is done and the customer is pleased:

- **Roofers:** at the final walkthrough, or the day after a repair holds through rain
- **Cleaners:** after the third or fourth visit of a recurring clean
- **HVAC and plumbing:** as soon as the system is running and the customer has seen it work
- **Lawn and pool:** after the first month of consistent service

Asking a day or two later still works. Asking a month later rarely does.

## Make it one tap

Get your review link from your Business Profile (look for "Ask for reviews" or "Get more reviews") and shorten it if you like. Then send it by text, because most people read texts within minutes.

## A script you can use

In person:

> "Really glad it turned out well. Reviews make a big difference for a small company like ours. Would you mind leaving one on Google? I'll text you the link. If you can, mention what we did and your town; it helps people nearby find us."

By text:

> "Hi [name], thanks again for having us out for [the job] today. If you have a minute, a Google review would help us a lot: [link]. Saying what we did and where really helps neighbors find us. Thanks, [your name]"

Short, personal and easy.

## What not to do

Google's policies are strict, and breaking them can get reviews removed or the profile penalized:

- **No incentives**: no discounts, gift cards or entries in a draw for reviews
- **No review gating**: don't ask only the customers you think are happy, or screen people before sending the link
- **No fake reviews**, from staff, family or bought services
- **No review stations** where customers post from your device at your office

## Reply to every review

Thank people by name and mention the job: "Thanks, Mark. Glad the new water heater is working well in your Viera home." For a bad review, reply calmly, once, with an offer to fix it offline. Future customers read the replies.

## Build it into the routine

Add the review ask to the end-of-job checklist, so it happens every time, not when someone remembers. Track how many reviews come in each month alongside your leads. For roofers specifically, see our [roofing reviews guide](/notes/google-reviews-for-roofers).
