Auto repair is one of the few local trades where customers come to you. That changes the SEO. When I went through [1,250 near-me searches](/notes/near-me-searches-local-seo-data) across five client sites, the businesses people visit in person won most of them: a pain clinic and a game shop accounted for 836. Near-me results lean hard on where the searcher is standing, and a shop has an address to measure from.

Repair shops aren't a trade I've worked in. Most of my sites are roofing, pool deck, lawn and shutter companies, plus that clinic and game shop. This is the setup I'd use for a shop, based on what worked for the storefront businesses.

## The Business Profile does most of the work

For a shop with a public address, the map pack is the main event.

- **Primary category: Auto repair shop.** Add others only for work you do a lot of, such as Brake shop, Oil change service or Transmission shop. [How to choose categories](/notes/google-business-profile-categories) explains the trade-offs.
- **Show your address.** Customers visit, so it should be on the profile.
- **Keep hours exact,** including holiday hours. A customer who drives over and finds the door locked leaves a review about it.
- **Fill in services** with the jobs you want more of.
- **Add real photos:** the front of the shop so people can find it, the bays, your team, the waiting area.

## A page for each service

People search the problem or the job: `brake repair`, `check engine light diagnostic`, `car AC repair`, `state inspection`. Build a page for each main service with:

- What the job involves and roughly how long it takes.
- What it usually costs, as a range, and what moves the price.
- The warning signs that bring people in ("grinding when I brake").
- A call button and a booking link near the top.

If you specialize in certain makes, such as Honda, Toyota or European cars, give each its own page. People search "[make] repair near me" when they want someone who knows their car.

## Trust: warranty, certifications, prices

Car owners worry about being upsold. Answer that on the site:

- **Your warranty,** in plain terms: how long, which parts and labor it covers, whether it's honored at other shops.
- **Certifications** such as ASE, listed by technician if you can.
- **How you quote,** including whether you call before doing extra work. A short estimates page helps.

## Reviews

Ask every customer for a review, the same way, with a direct link on the receipt or in a follow-up text. Google prohibits incentives for reviews, so no discount for a rating. Reply to every negative review with specifics and what you did about it. People choosing a shop read the replies. [Getting more Google reviews](/notes/google-reviews-for-roofers) is written for roofers, and the method works the same.

## Schema and the basics

Add AutoRepair structured data to your homepage or contact page: name, address, phone, hours, and the area you serve. Schema.org has the AutoRepair type built for this. [Roofing contractor schema](/notes/roofing-contractor-schema) shows the same kind of markup for a different trade.

Keep your shop's name, address and phone identical on the site, the profile and the main directories.

## Seasonal pages

Car problems follow the weather: AC repair in late spring, batteries, tires and heaters as it gets cold. Publish or refresh those pages a couple of months before the season, since new pages take time to be indexed. Search Console's Performance report, compared against last year, shows when your searches rise.

## Track calls and bookings

Most repair customers call. Track website phone taps as a GA4 key event, count Business Profile calls separately, and make online bookings their own event. [Call tracking for contractors](/notes/call-tracking-for-contractors) covers how to keep the sources apart.
