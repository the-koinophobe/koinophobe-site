Most roofing SEO advice is written as if every roof sat in the same weather. Florida isn't that market. Hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30, insurance shapes most replacement decisions, and the building code is stricter than in most states. All of that changes what homeowners type into Google, and when.

We've done the SEO on several roofing sites in Brevard County. Here's what we'd put first for a Florida roofer.

## The searches are different here

Outside Florida, roofing searches are mostly "roof repair near me" and "roof replacement cost." Florida has those too, plus a set most out-of-state agencies miss:

- **Storm searches.** "Roof leak after hurricane," "emergency roof tarp," "wind damage roof inspection." These spike for days after a storm and then vanish.
- **Insurance searches.** "Roof insurance claim help," "will insurance pay for a new roof," "roof inspection for insurance." Homeowners are trying to understand a process, not hire yet.
- **Code and permit searches.** Questions about permits, inspections and what's required on a replacement. Answer them plainly and point to your county's building department for the official word.
- **Roof type searches.** Tile, metal and shingle each have their own buyers. A homeowner pricing a metal roof is a different lead from one patching shingles.

Each of these deserves its own page. One "Services" page can't rank for all of them.

## The pages a Florida roofing site needs

1. **A page for each service.** Repair, replacement, inspections, and each roof type you install. Our [roof replacement page guide](/notes/roof-replacement-service-page) covers what goes on one.
2. **A page for each town you want work in.** With jobs you've done there, not the same page with the town name swapped. See [roofing service area pages](/notes/roofing-service-area-pages).
3. **Storm pages, written before the storm.** A page that already exists and has been crawled can rank when a storm hits; one written the morning after usually can't. [Storm damage pages](/notes/storm-damage-roofing-pages) covers the build.
4. **An insurance claim explainer.** What a homeowner should do, in order, after damage. Be careful with promises: explain the process, don't guarantee an outcome.

## The Business Profile matters more after a storm

When a storm passes, map pack searches jump and every roofer in the county gets calls. The profiles that win are the ones that look current: recent photos, recent reviews, hours that are true, and posts that say what you're doing this week.

Two things to set up now, while it's quiet:

- **The right categories.** "Roofing contractor" as primary, with secondary categories only where you really do the work. Our [roofer's Business Profile guide](/notes/google-business-profile-for-roofers) goes through them.
- **A review habit.** Ask every finished job for a review that mentions the town and the work. Reviews with those words help both the map and AI answers.

## Watch out for storm chasers in your own results

After a storm, out-of-area companies set up temporary listings and pages. Some use addresses they don't have. If a listing near you is fake, [report it](/notes/report-fake-google-business-listings). Don't copy their tactics: Google suspends profiles with virtual addresses, and a suspension in hurricane season costs more than any ranking.

## Track calls before you spend more

Storm months bring calls that would have come anyway. Without tracking, you can't tell what the SEO did. Set up [call tracking](/notes/call-tracking-for-contractors) so every month's report separates calls from Google, from the Business Profile and from everything else.

## What results look like

On one Brevard County roofing site, average Google position went from 47.9 to 14.9 year on year, and "roofing melbourne fl" went from about position 77 to 12.9. It took town pages, profile work and patience. The full numbers are on the [work page](/work), and how we run it for roofers is on the [roofing SEO page](/roofing-seo).

## Next step

List the five towns you most want work in and check whether your site has a page for each. If it doesn't, that's your first month of work.
