Your primary category is one of the strongest signals your Business Profile sends. Changing it can be the single best fix for a profile that shows up for the wrong searches, and one of the riskiest edits you can make right before a busy season.

## When a change makes sense

- **The category doesn't match most of your revenue.** A company that mostly replaces roofs but leads with "General contractor."
- **Your business has changed.** You started in lawn care and now most of your revenue is landscape installs.
- **Competitors winning your searches all use a different primary category**, and it fits what you do better.

When it doesn't: chasing a slightly different wording of the same thing, or adding a trade you don't really do. See [choosing your categories](/notes/google-business-profile-categories) and [categories by trade](/notes/gbp-categories-by-trade).

## Before you change it

1. **Write down a baseline.** Last month's profile calls, website clicks and directions, and the same month last year, from the [performance report](/notes/google-business-profile-performance-report).
2. **Note your positions** for your main searches in the map. Search from a few places in your area and write down where you appear.
3. **Pick a quiet time.** Not the week before storm season, summer AC rush or spring lawn sign-ups.
4. **Change only the category.** Don't change your name, address or phone at the same time. Several edits together are more likely to trigger a review.

## Making the change

In your profile, open **Edit profile > Business category**, set the new primary category, and decide what happens to the old one. If the old category still describes real work, keep it as an additional category rather than dropping it.

Then update your **services** to match the new primary category. Some services are tied to specific categories, so check the list after the change.

## What to watch afterwards

- **Week one:** check the profile is still live and verified. If Google asks for re-verification, do it straight away.
- **Weeks two to six:** compare profile calls and searches against your baseline, and against the same weeks last year.
- **Map positions:** search your main terms from the same places as before.

A small dip in the first week or two can happen while Google re-evaluates the profile. If calls are still well down after a month, with no seasonal reason, consider switching back.

## Match the website

The service your new primary category describes should have a strong page on your site: the one you link from the profile, or one linked prominently from your homepage. A category with nothing behind it sends people to a site that doesn't confirm it.

## Keep a change log

Note every profile edit with a date: categories, services, hours, photos. When numbers move, the log tells you why. It's also useful if the profile is ever suspended and you need to explain changes. See [suspended profiles](/notes/google-business-profile-suspended).

## Next step

Compare your primary category with the service most of your revenue comes from. If they don't match, take a baseline today and plan the change for your next quiet week.
