Most of the sites I work on are roofing, pool deck, lawn and shutter companies. Pressure washing is close to pool deck work, since both deal with the concrete around a house. I don't have a pressure washing client, but the searches follow the same rules, so this is the setup I'd build. Results I mention come from the trades I do work in.

## How people search for it

Pressure washing searches are mostly about a surface. Someone notices the driveway has gone dark, the siding has green streaks, or the deck is slippery. They search `driveway cleaning [town]`, `house washing near me` or `roof cleaning [town]`, look at photos, and ask two or three companies for a price.

Those searches name a job, so a single "Services" page listing everything gives Google little to match for any of them. Near-me searches matter too. Across five sites I work on, 1,250 different near-me queries showed up between April 2025 and August 2026, with 780 of them on page one. My [near-me search data](/notes/near-me-searches-local-seo-data) note breaks that down.

## A page for each service

Build a page for each job you want more of:

![A worker using a high pressure power washer to clean concrete seating](/notes/photos/pressure-washing-seo-1.webp "Photo: Infinara on Unsplash")

| Page | What it should answer |
|---|---|
| House washing / soft washing | Siding types you clean, the method and pressure you use, what you do to protect plants and windows |
| Driveway and concrete cleaning | Driveways, sidewalks and patios, oil and rust stains, sealing if you offer it |
| Roof cleaning | The method you use on shingles or tile, how long results last, roof types you won't touch |
| Deck cleaning | Wood and composite, whether you stain or seal afterward |

Roof cleaning needs the most explanation. Some homeowners worry that washing will damage the roof, so if you soft wash roofs, say so and describe how. If you also do gutters, fences or commercial buildings, those can be their own pages later. If you also clean inside homes, [house cleaning SEO](/notes/house-cleaning-seo) covers how those searches and pages differ.

Each page needs a title tag naming the service and your main town, a tap-to-call button near the top, your own photos, and the questions customers ask you before they book.

## Before and after photos do the selling

Pressure washing shows up well in photos. A clean half of a driveway next to a dirty half shows a customer what they'd be paying for.

Take a photo from the same spot before you start and after you finish. Put each pair on the matching service page and on the matching town page, with a few lines under it: the surface, the town, what was on it and how long it took. Rename the files before uploading (`driveway-cleaning-[town]-before.webp`), compress them, and write plain alt text. My [before and after photos](/notes/before-after-gallery-seo) note has the full routine. On my own site, photos went from about 3 MB JPEGs to WebP files of 93 to 200 KB at 1200 pixels, which keeps a photo-heavy page fast on a phone.

Add the same photos to your Google Business Profile as jobs finish, so they show in the map results too.

## A quote form that gets you a price fast

Most pressure washing jobs start with a quote, so the quote form is where the SEO turns into work. Keep it short: name, phone, email, town, the surfaces to clean, and a photo upload. A picture of the house or driveway lets you price a lot of jobs from the office. Say when the customer will hear back, and keep that promise. The [free estimate page](/notes/lawrence-ma-free-estimate-page) note covers the rest of what that page needs.

Then make sure it still sends. In July 2026 I found a client's form submissions had been dropping since March because of an email setting, and nobody noticed for four months, me included. I now test form delivery on every site every month.

## Seasonal timing

In much of the country, outdoor cleaning picks up in spring and early summer, and your region decides the exact weeks. Your own data is the better guide. Open the Performance report in Google Search Console, compare the last few months with the same months a year ago, and note when your service searches rose. New pages can take weeks or months to be indexed and settle in rankings, so publish or refresh each page a couple of months ahead of that rise. The [seasonal content calendar](/notes/seasonal-content-calendar-home-services) shows how I plan it across trades.

## Town pages, reviews and tracking

A page for each town you want work in can help with `pressure washing [town]` searches. Each needs something true about that town, such as jobs you've done there with photos. Near-identical pages with the town name swapped count as doorway spam under Google's policies.

Ask every customer for a review, the same way, soon after the job. Google prohibits incentives and asking only the happy ones, and the FTC's fake reviews rule (in effect since October 21, 2024) allows civil penalties up to $51,744 per violation.

Track website phone taps and quote form submissions as GA4 key events, and count Business Profile calls separately, so you can see which service pages bring in work.
