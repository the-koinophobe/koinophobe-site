Your primary category tells Google what you are. Secondary categories, the up-to-nine extras, tell it what else you do. Most contractors either skip them or fill all nine slots with anything close. Both cost calls.

## What secondary categories actually do

They make your profile eligible for searches related to that category. A roofer who adds "Gutter cleaning service" can appear for gutter searches nearby, if the rest of the profile backs it up: photos, reviews, services and a website page about gutters.

What they don't do is replace the primary category. The primary category carries the most weight. If you're unsure which to lead with, read [choosing your Business Profile categories](/notes/google-business-profile-categories) first.

## When to add one

Add a secondary category when all three are true:

1. **You do the work every month**, not once a year.
2. **You have a page on your site about it.** The category points people to a service; the page confirms it.
3. **You can show it.** Photos of the work and at least a few reviews that mention it.

## When to leave one out

- **Services you'd like to offer someday.** Calls you can't take do nothing for you.
- **Near-duplicates of your primary.** "Roofing contractor" plus "Roofing service" and "Roof repair service" rarely adds anything; pick the one that fits and move on.
- **Unrelated trades.** A plumber adding "Electrician" because a cousin does wiring sends mixed signals and can get the profile edited.
- **Categories that describe your customers.** "Property management company" isn't your category because you serve property managers.

## How many is right

For most home service businesses, two to five secondary categories. A pool service company might use pool cleaning as primary and add pool repair and pool contractor. A cleaning company might lead with house cleaning and add carpet cleaning and commercial cleaning. Our [categories by trade list](/notes/gbp-categories-by-trade) has suggestions for the main trades.

## How to test whether a category is working

Google doesn't report performance per category, so test the slow way:

1. **Note your baseline.** Profile calls and searches for the related service, from the Performance tab and Search Console.
2. **Add one category at a time**, together with a matching page and a few photos.
3. **Wait four to six weeks**, then compare. Did searches mentioning that service rise? Did calls about it come in?
4. **Remove it if nothing moved** after a reasonable wait. A category that brings nothing is just noise.

Changing many things at once makes it impossible to tell what worked.

## A note on category changes

Big category edits can trigger a review of your profile, and in some cases a temporary drop. Make changes one at a time, keep a record of what you changed and when, and avoid editing everything right before your busy season.

## Next step

Open your profile and list your secondary categories. Cross out any that fail the three tests above, and for the ones that pass, check you have a matching page. Our [local SEO checklist](/notes/local-seo-checklist-home-services) covers the rest of the profile.
