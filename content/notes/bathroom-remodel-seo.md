Nobody remodels a bathroom on impulse. Homeowners save photos, read about costs, compare tile and showers, and ask friends. By the time they call, they've usually picked two or three companies whose work they've already seen online.

Our [home remodeling SEO page](/home-remodeling-seo) covers the whole approach for remodelers. This note is about the bathroom pages specifically.

## A bathroom remodeling page of its own

If bathrooms are a big part of your work, they need their own page, not a line on a general remodeling page. It should cover:

- What a bathroom remodel with you includes, from design to final walkthrough
- Typical timelines, and what can extend them
- How you handle permits, plumbing and electrical
- How to start: a consultation, what to prepare

## Project pages, one per bathroom

The most convincing content a remodeler can publish is a page for each finished bathroom:

- Before and after photos
- Town and neighborhood
- Scope: what changed, what was moved, materials used
- Timeline and, if the client agrees, a budget range
- A quote from the homeowner

These pages rank for specific searches and give homeowners proof. We explain the approach in the [before and after gallery guide](/notes/before-after-gallery-seo).

## Pages for specific projects

Homeowners search for specific jobs:

- Walk-in showers
- Tub-to-shower conversions
- Accessible and aging-in-place bathrooms
- Small bathroom remodels
- Primary bath remodels
- Vanity and tile updates

Each can be a page linking to matching projects.

## An honest cost page

"How much does a bathroom remodel cost" is the most searched question in this part of the trade. Ranges from your own projects, by size and scope, with the factors that move the price, rank for it and bring better-prepared leads.

## AI assistants and remodeling

More homeowners ask ChatGPT and other assistants about remodeling costs and who to hire. Clear cost pages, project pages and consistent business details make you easier to name. Our [home remodeling SEO page](/home-remodeling-seo) covers what we've measured.

## Business Profile

List bathroom remodeling as a service, add project photos monthly, and ask for reviews at the final walkthrough that mention the bathroom and the town. Our [review script](/notes/how-to-ask-for-google-reviews) has wording.

## Track consultations

The lead is a booked consultation. Count consultation requests and calls by page so you know which project pages bring business. See [form tracking in GA4](/notes/ga4-form-tracking-contractors).

## Next step

Turn your last five finished bathrooms into project pages with photos, scope and town. Then check you have an honest cost page. Those two do more for bathroom leads than anything else on the site.
