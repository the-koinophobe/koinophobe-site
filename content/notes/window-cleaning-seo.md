Window cleaning is one of the easiest services to sell online, and one of the easiest to lose on price. People can see the result, so photos and reviews do a lot of the work. The businesses that grow turn a spring clean into a quarterly route.

## Split the services

- **Residential window cleaning**, inside and out
- **Commercial and storefront cleaning**, often recurring
- **High-rise or multi-story work**, if you have the equipment and insurance
- **Screens and tracks**
- **Hard water stain removal**, a common search in some regions
- **Add-ons** such as gutter cleaning or pressure washing, if you do them

Give each its own page, with what's included, what it costs and how long it takes.

## Pricing people can understand

Publish a starting price, or price by number of windows, panes or home size. Say what costs more: second and third stories, screens, tracks, heavy buildup, hard-to-reach windows. Clear pricing cuts down on calls that don't book and makes you easy to choose.

## Photos and reviews do the selling

Before and after photos of real windows, captioned with the town and type of job. Ask for a review right after the job, when the customer is looking through clean glass. Reviews that mention punctuality and care count, because you're working around people's homes. Our [review request script](/notes/how-to-ask-for-google-reviews) has wording you can use.

## Business Profile

- **Primary category:** Window cleaning service
- **Secondary categories** only for work you do, such as gutter cleaning or pressure washing. See [categories by trade](/notes/gbp-categories-by-trade).
- **Photos:** before and after, your team, your vehicle
- **Hours** that match when you answer the phone

## Routes and town pages

Window cleaning is route work: the more customers on the same streets, the better the margins. Build pages for the towns and neighborhoods where you already work and want more. Mention the kinds of homes and buildings you clean there. See [service area pages](/notes/roofing-service-area-pages).

## Commercial pages bring recurring work

Storefronts, offices and restaurants book monthly or quarterly. A commercial page that explains scheduling, after-hours work, insurance and invoicing helps you win contracts that keep routes full.

## Remind and rebook

After each residential job, offer a recurring schedule and send reminders. It's the cheapest growth you'll find, and it makes every customer SEO brings in worth much more.

## Next step

Search "window cleaning [your town]" and look at the top three. If they show prices and you don't, add them. Then check you have separate residential and commercial pages. If you also pressure wash, see [pressure washing SEO](/notes/pressure-washing-seo).
