The roofing SEO pitches you get by email run from $199 a month to $5,000. The cheap ones can't do much real work at that price, and the expensive ones are often built for companies ten times your size. Here's what a small roofer's budget should actually buy.

For what the wider market charges, see [what roofing SEO costs](/notes/roofing-seo-cost). This note is about getting the most from a smaller budget.

## What a lean budget has to cover

In order of what pays back first:

1. **Tracking.** Calls and forms counted in GA4, so you know what's working. It's a one-time setup and cheap to keep running. See [call tracking for contractors](/notes/call-tracking-for-contractors).
2. **The Business Profile.** Categories, services, photos, a review habit and regular posts. For a local roofer, the map often brings more calls than the website.
3. **One or two pages a month.** A service page or a town page each month adds up to a site that covers your area within a year.
4. **Fixes.** Broken forms, slow pages, indexing problems. Most small roofing sites have a few.
5. **A one-page report.** Leads, profile actions, clicks and what was done. Here's [our template](/notes/monthly-seo-report-template).

## What you can skip on a small budget

- **Big link building campaigns.** For a local roofer, a few real local links (suppliers, associations, community sponsorships) beat hundreds of bought ones.
- **Blog posts nobody local reads.** General roofing articles attract readers across the country. Town and service pages bring the calls.
- **Paid tool dashboards.** Your own Search Console and GA4 are free and more accurate for your site.

## Red flags in cheap offers

- **"Guaranteed page one."** Nobody controls Google.
- **No list of work.** If you can't see what was done, assume very little was.
- **Long contracts at low prices.** A cheap price locked in for a year is how low-effort agencies keep clients.
- **They want to own your accounts.** Your Business Profile and analytics should always be in your name.
