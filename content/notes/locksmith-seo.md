**The short answer:** locksmith SEO is mostly about looking and being real. A verified Business Profile with your actual business name and a local number, pages for the emergencies people search (car lockout, house lockout, rekey, lock change), a clear price structure, and a phone that gets answered. Then report the fake listings around you, because every one you get removed is a competitor gone.

## The problem nobody else in home services has as badly

Locksmith results have been a target for fake listings for years. A 2017 study by Google and UC San Diego on fraudulent Maps listings found locksmiths among the categories hit hardest. The pattern is still common: a listing with a generic name and a local-looking number, answered by a call center that quotes low and sends whoever's free. The customer pays double and blames "locksmiths".

That's your opening. Everything a fake listing can't do, you can: a real address or a real service area, a real name on the van, photos of real jobs, reviews that mention your techs by name, and a price structure you stick to.

## The Business Profile

- **Name:** your actual registered business name. No "24/7 Locksmith [City] Cheap" stuffing; it's against Google's guidelines and it's exactly what fakes do.
- **Primary category:** **Locksmith**. Add others only for work you sell, like **Safe & vault shop** or **Key duplication service**.
- **Service area:** if you work from a van and customers don't come to you, hide the address and list the towns you cover. [Service area businesses on Google](/notes/service-area-business-google-business-profile) covers the rules.
- **Hours:** if you take emergency calls overnight, say so, and answer them. A profile that claims 24 hours and goes to voicemail at 2am gets bad reviews fast.
- **Photos:** your van, your techs, real jobs. They're proof a call center can't fake.

## The pages to build

People searching for a locksmith are usually standing outside a locked door. They search the problem, not the trade:

- **Car lockout.** Which vehicles you handle, how fast you usually arrive, whether you cut and program car keys and fobs.
- **House lockout.** Arrival time, what ID you need to see before opening a home, and that you don't drill unless you have to.
- **Rekeying and lock changes.** The big one after a move or a breakup. Explain the difference and the price of each.
- **Commercial work.** Master key systems, access control, panic bars, if you do them. These are planned purchases with bigger tickets.
- **Safes,** if you open or install them.

Keep each page short and phone-first: the number at the top, a tap-to-call button that works, and the answer to "how soon can you get here?" near the top.

## Prices and proof

Fakes win on a low phone quote and lose at the door. Beat them on the page: your service call fee, how labor is charged, and typical ranges for a lockout, a rekey and a lock change. Say you give the full price before starting work.

Show your license and insurance. Some states license locksmiths; if yours does, put the license number in the footer of every page. It's one more thing a call center can't copy.

## Answer the phone

Locksmith calls don't wait. If a call goes to voicemail, the next result gets the job within a minute. [Missed calls](/notes/missed-calls-contractors) covers text-back and routing, and [call tracking for contractors](/notes/call-tracking-for-contractors) shows how to count which pages ring the phone.

## Report the fakes

Search "locksmith [your town]" and "locksmith near me" from a few spots in your area. Look for listings with no website, a name that's just keywords, a pin on a house or an empty lot, or photos lifted from stock sites. Report each one through Google's tools. [Reporting fake Google Business listings](/notes/report-fake-google-business-listings) walks through it, including the redressal form for persistent ones.

It's slow, unglamorous work, and it moves you up the map pack without changing anything on your own listing.
