**The short answer:** verification usually fails because what's on the profile doesn't match what's in the video. The name on the profile has to match your van, sign or paperwork. The video has to be one continuous take showing where you are, the business itself, and proof that you run it. And you shouldn't touch the profile while it's pending.

Google decides which verification method you get: video, a live video call, a phone or text code, email, or, less often now, a postcard. For most new home service profiles I see, it's video.

## What Google wants to see

Google's prompts during the upload say what it needs for your case, so read them before you film. In general, a verification video shows three things in one take:

1. **Where you are.** A street sign, the building's outside, or nearby landmarks. For a service business with no shop, film from where you base the business.
2. **The business.** Signage, a branded vehicle, your tools and equipment, stock, a work order.
3. **That you run it.** Unlock the door or the van, open the tool cabinet, show business documents like a registration, license or insurance certificate with the business name on it.

Keep it to one continuous recording. Don't cut, edit, add music or switch between clips. A minute or two is usually plenty.

## If you work from a van, not a shop

Service area businesses get rejected more often, mostly because there's nothing obviously "business" to film at a house. Things that help:

- **A branded vehicle,** with the business name matching the profile exactly. Film the name, then walk around it and open it up.
- **Your tools and equipment,** in use or ready to go.
- **Paperwork with the business name:** license, insurance certificate, registration, a recent invoice.

Set the profile to hide your address and list your service areas before you submit. [Service area businesses on Google](/notes/service-area-business-google-business-profile) covers that setup.

## The mistakes that get videos rejected

**The name doesn't match.** If your profile says "Smith Roofing Best Roofer Near Me" and your van says "Smith Roofing", Google sees a keyword-stuffed name that doesn't match the business. Use your real business name, the one on your paperwork.

**Nothing in the video proves it's a business.** A driveway and a front door could be anyone's house.

**Edited or stitched clips.** It has to be one take.

**Editing the profile while it's pending.** Changing the name, category or address mid-review can restart the process or trigger a new one. Leave it alone until it's done.

**The category doesn't fit.** If your primary category says one trade and your video shows another, that's a mismatch too. [Business Profile categories](/notes/google-business-profile-categories) covers picking the right one.

## While it's pending

Reviews can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks. Don't create a second profile to get around it; duplicates cause more trouble than waiting does. Do use the time to write your services list, gather 10 to 20 real job photos, and get your website's contact page matching the profile's name, phone and service area.

## If it's rejected

Read the message, fix the mismatch it points to, and try again with a better video. If you're offered a different method, take it. If you're stuck in a loop, Google's Business Profile support can review it; have your license, insurance and registration ready to send.

If the profile was verified before and has now been suspended, that's a different problem. [Suspended profiles](/notes/google-business-profile-suspended) covers it.

## Where to start

Before you film, check three things: the profile name matches your paperwork exactly, the address and service area settings fit how you actually work, and you've got your van, tools and documents ready to show in one take.
