In most of the country, lawn care is a spring decision. The first warm weekend, people look at the yard, search "lawn care near me", and sign up with whoever they find. Many stay with that company for the season, and some for years.

That makes timing the whole game for a lawn care company's website. Pages take weeks to months to start ranking. If you want calls in March, the pages need to be live by December.

I work on a lawn and landscaping site in Florida, where the season barely stops. The setup is the same either way.

## A page for each service

People search for the specific job:

![A lawn care worker trimming grass along a sidewalk in front of a house](/notes/photos/lawn-care-seo-1.webp "Photo: Jared Muller on Unsplash")

- lawn mowing service
- aeration and overseeding
- fertilization and weed control
- sod installation
- shrub trimming and mulching
- leaf cleanup

Each one gets its own page that says what's included, how often you do it, when in the year it should happen, and roughly what it costs. "Aeration" and "fertilization" are different searches from "lawn care", and a single services page can't rank for all of them.

## A page for your plans

A lot of lawn care revenue is recurring. A page that lays out your plans side by side (weekly mowing, the seasonal program, what each includes, what each costs) answers the question most people have before they call, and it's a page people bookmark and share with a spouse.

If you publish prices, you'll get fewer calls from people who were never going to buy, which saves your time for the ones who will.

## Town pages, with real yards

Lawn care is very local. Soil, grass type, HOA rules and lot sizes change from one town to the next. A page for each town you serve, with photos of yards you maintain there and a few lines about what that area needs, does better than a list of town names. I wrote about how to build these in [service area pages](/notes/roofing-service-area-pages). It's written for roofers, and the method is the same.

## The map pack

"Lawn care near me" is decided mostly by the map. Get the Business Profile right: real name, the right primary category, your service area as towns (up to 20, within roughly two hours of driving), services listed, photos added every month. The [Business Profile setup guide](/notes/google-business-profile-for-roofers) covers each step.

Reviews matter a lot here because lawn care is a trust purchase: someone is going to be in your yard every week. Ask every new customer after their first month, when the lawn looks noticeably better. [Getting more Google reviews](/notes/google-reviews-for-roofers) covers how, within Google's rules.

## A winter plan

- **November:** service pages and the plans page.
- **December to January:** town pages for your five best areas.
- **February:** Business Profile cleanup, new photos, a review push with existing customers.
- **March onward:** watch which pages bring calls, and add photos as the season starts.
