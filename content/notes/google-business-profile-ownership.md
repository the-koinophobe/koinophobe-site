**The short answer:** open your Business Profile settings and look under **People and access**. Your business, on a Google account you control, should be the primary owner. Anyone helping you, me included, should be a manager. If someone else owns it, request access through Google, and if they won't hand it over, Google's claim process is how you get it back.

Whoever owns the profile owns the reviews, the photos, the call history and the ranking you've built. I ask about it in the first week with every client, because when it's wrong, fixing it comes before anything else. It's one of my [onboarding questions](/notes/seo-onboarding-questions) for that reason.

## How it goes wrong

Three ways I see over and over:

**The agency set it up.** A past marketing company created the profile under its own Google account. Fine while you work together. When you stop, they still hold the keys.

**An employee set it up.** Someone in the office created it with a personal Gmail, then left.

**It used to be someone else's listing.** A profile reused from an old business at the same address, or a phone number that once belonged to another company. In one audit I did in November 2026, a top review on a repair company's profile praised the food and drinks. That's a sign the profile has history the current owner may not know about, and a profile that changed businesses can get suspended.

## Check who owns it now

1. Sign in to the Google account you believe owns the profile.
2. Search for your business name on Google, or open Google Maps, and open the profile's management menu.
3. Go to **Settings**, then **People and access**.

You'll see each person and their role. If your account isn't listed as primary owner, that's the problem to fix.

| Role | Can edit the profile | Can add or remove people | How many |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary owner | Yes | Yes, including transferring primary ownership | One |
| Owner | Yes | Yes | Any number |
| Manager | Yes | No | Any number |

## If you can reach the current owner

Ask them to add your business Google account as an owner, then transfer primary ownership to it. Primary ownership can only go to someone who's already an owner, so it's two steps. Once you're primary owner, set them to manager or remove them.

Do it on a call while you both have the settings open. It takes five minutes and saves a week of emails.

## If you can't

Search for your business on Google, choose the option to claim or manage the profile, and request access. Google emails the current owner, who can approve or deny the request. If they don't answer within Google's window, you may be given a way to verify the business yourself and take control. Google lists the current timings and steps on its help page; they've changed before, so check there rather than trusting a number from an old blog post.

While you wait, don't create a second profile for the same business. Duplicates split your reviews and can get both listings flagged. [Fake and duplicate listings](/notes/report-fake-google-business-listings) covers how Google handles them.

## Set it up so it never happens again

- **Use a business Google account,** not someone's personal Gmail. An address on your own domain is best.
- **Keep two owners,** you and one other person you trust, so one lost login doesn't lock you out.
- **Give agencies manager access.** Managers can do all the day-to-day work. They just can't take the profile with them.
- **Do the same for Search Console and Google Analytics.** Same rule: the business owns, helpers are added as users.

## When ownership changes, check the basics

After you take back control, read the profile top to bottom: business name, primary category, phone number, website link, hours and service areas. People who set up a profile for you sometimes point the website link at their own tracking page or a landing page they control. [Business Profile categories](/notes/google-business-profile-categories) covers the category, and [service area businesses on Google](/notes/service-area-business-google-business-profile) covers the address and areas.
