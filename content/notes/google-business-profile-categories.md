**The short answer:** pick the most specific primary category that matches the work most of your money comes from. Add one to four more only for work you actually do and want calls for. Check what the top three map results use for your main search before you decide.

Of all the settings on a Google Business Profile, the primary category is the one I check first. It tells Google what kind of business you are, and that decides which searches your profile can show up for. A roofer listed as "Contractor" competes for a vague set of searches. Listed as "Roofing contractor", it competes for the ones that bring roof jobs.

Local SEO practitioners agree on this more than on most things. In Whitespark's Local Search Ranking Factors survey, the primary category comes out as the most important factor for the map pack.

## Where to find the full category list

Google doesn't publish a list you can download. The list lives inside the profile editor: open **Edit profile**, go to **Business category**, and start typing. Type "roof" and you'll see every category with roof in it; type "clean" and you'll see the cleaning ones. That's the real, current list.

Lists copied onto other websites go stale, because Google adds and removes categories over time. Use them for ideas, then confirm in the editor. If you run a home service business, I've put the primary and secondary picks for each trade in [Business Profile categories by trade](/notes/gbp-categories-by-trade).

## How categories work

You get one primary category and up to nine additional ones. You pick them from Google's list; you can't type your own. Google's guidelines ask you to use the fewest categories that describe your core business, and not to use categories as keywords or to list services.

The primary category carries the most weight. Additional categories tell Google you're also relevant for other kinds of searches. They help, just less.

## Choosing the primary category

Pick the most specific category that describes the work most of your revenue comes from.

- A roofer who also does gutters: **Roofing contractor**, with gutters as an additional category.
- An HVAC company: **HVAC contractor**, or **Air conditioning contractor** if most calls are for cooling.
- A kitchen and bath remodeler: **Kitchen remodeler** or **Bathroom remodeler**, whichever brings in more of the revenue, with the other one and **Remodeler** as additional categories. The rest of that setup is on my [home remodeling SEO](/home-remodeling-seo) page.
- A cleaning company: **House cleaning service** if you mainly clean homes, **Commercial cleaning service** if you mainly clean offices.

If two categories fit, search for your main service in your town and look at what the top three map results use as their primary. That's a strong hint about what Google associates with the search.

## Adding the rest

Add a category only if you do that work and want those calls. Each one should match a real service with a page on your website. A pool deck company that adds "Swimming pool contractor" when it doesn't build pools will get calls it can't take.

Most local businesses need two to five categories in total. If you're at nine, check whether some of them describe services rather than business types. Services belong in the Services section of the profile.

## How to see what competitors use

The primary category shows under the business name in Google Maps and in the search results panel. Additional categories don't show publicly, but local SEO browser extensions and rank tracking tools can read them from the page.

Look at the top three businesses for your main search in two or three towns you serve. My [near-me search data](/notes/near-me-searches-local-seo-data) shows how much the results change with where the searcher stands. If they all share a primary category you don't use, that's worth testing.

## Changing categories

Category changes can send a profile for review or trigger a new verification step, so change one thing at a time and wait a few weeks before judging the result. If your work is seasonal, some practitioners switch the primary category with the season, such as an HVAC company moving between air conditioning and heating. That works best when both categories are true all year.

Check for new categories every few months, too. Google adds them, and a better fit for your business may not have existed when you set the profile up.

## Where to start

Open your profile, read your primary category, and search your main service in your home town. If the top three use something different, look closely at why.
