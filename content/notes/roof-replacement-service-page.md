A homeowner looking at a roof replacement is about to spend a lot of money on something they'll buy maybe once or twice in their life. They read slowly. They compare. Your page should answer their questions in roughly the order they ask them.

On a roofing site I work on, `roof replacement melbourne fl` went from position 70 to 15, comparing October 2025 to August 2026 with the eleven months before. That happened alongside site-wide work: title and meta rewrites, a service page per town, FAQ and service schema, and articles on the questions Florida homeowners ask after storms. The order below is how I'd lay out a replacement page.

## What you replace, and with what

Open with the job in plain words. Full tear-off or overlay, and which roof types you install: architectural shingles, metal, tile, flat roof systems. List only what your crews put on. If you don't do tile, leave tile off.

Name the brands you install if you use the same ones on most jobs. Some homeowners search by brand.

## Signs it's time to replace instead of repair

This is the question that brings a lot of people to the page. Give them honest signs: the roof's age, shingles curling or missing across large areas, granules collecting in the gutters, soft spots in the decking, leaks that keep coming back after repairs.

Then say when a repair is enough. Saying you'd fix a small leak instead of selling a whole roof makes the rest of the page easier to believe. Link to your repair page from here.

## The process, day by day

Walk through a typical job the way your crews work it. Inspection and estimate. Permit. Material delivery. Tear-off. Decking check and any board replacement. Underlayment, then the new roof. Cleanup and a magnet sweep for nails. Final inspection.

If most of your replacements take a day or two, say so. If weather or permits often stretch that, say that too.

## What drives the cost

Skip the made-up averages. List what changes your price: roof size, pitch, material, how many layers come off, how much decking needs replacing, and access to the roof. Tell them how you quote, whether it's a free inspection, a written estimate or both.

If you want to publish a range, pull it from your own recent jobs and keep it current.

## Warranties and certifications, if they're real

If you hold a manufacturer certification, name it and link to the manufacturer's page that confirms it. Spell out your workmanship warranty and the manufacturer's material warranty separately, because homeowners mix them up. If you don't have either, leave this section out. Don't borrow language from a competitor's page.

## Financing, if you offer it

One short section: who the lender is, how to apply and a link. If you don't offer financing, skip it.

## Photos from your own jobs

Use photos of roofs your crews did, in the towns you serve. Before and after shots of the same house are the most useful. Stock photos of a smiling roofer don't help anyone decide. Compress them and name the files properly, which I cover in [image optimization for contractor websites](/notes/image-optimization-contractor-websites).

## Towns you serve

List the towns, and link each to its own page if that page has real local content. My note on [roofing service area pages](/notes/roofing-service-area-pages) covers how to build those without running into Google's doorway rules.

## FAQs

Answer the questions your office gets on the phone: how long it takes, whether they need to be home, what happens if it rains mid-job, whether insurance will cover storm damage. Mark them up with FAQPage schema if the answers are on the page. Google stopped showing FAQ dropdowns for most sites in 2023. The markup still labels the content clearly. More in [roofing contractor schema](/notes/roofing-contractor-schema).

## A way to book

End every section with a path forward, and finish the page with a clear one: a phone number that's tappable on mobile, a short form and online booking if you can take it. On a roofing site I work on, a search about booking an inspection ranked at 2.6 and got zero clicks in four weeks. The page had no way to book online. I wrote that up in [ranking but no clicks](/notes/zero-click-rankings-title-tags).

Then track it. Calls and form fills from this page should show up as key events in GA4, or you won't know whether the rewrite worked.
