People don't choose a cleaner the way they choose a plumber. Nobody's house is flooding. They're letting a stranger into their home, often every week or two, so they read reviews, compare prices and look for signs the company is careful. Your search presence has to answer those worries before they call.

We see searches like "house cleaning seo" and "cleaning company seo" in our own Search Console, and the same few gaps on most cleaning sites. Here's how we'd close them.

## The Business Profile comes first

For "house cleaning near me" and "cleaning service [town]," the map pack usually sits above the regular results. Get the profile right:

- **Primary category** that matches your main revenue: house cleaning service for homes, janitorial service for commercial. Secondary categories only for work you do. Our [categories by trade list](/notes/gbp-categories-by-trade) covers cleaning.
- **Service area** set to the towns you actually drive to. Most cleaning businesses don't have a storefront, so hide the address and set a service area instead. See [service area businesses on Google](/notes/service-area-business-google-business-profile).
- **Services listed** one by one: standard cleaning, deep cleaning, move-out cleaning, recurring cleaning.
- **Photos** of real work, real staff and branded uniforms or vehicles. Trust is the product.

## A page for every service

One "Services" page can't rank for everything a homeowner searches. Build separate pages:

1. **Recurring house cleaning** (weekly, biweekly, monthly)
2. **Deep cleaning**, with what's included room by room
3. **Move-in and move-out cleaning**, which has its own urgent, high-intent searches
4. **Commercial or office cleaning**, if you do it
5. **Add-ons** that people search for directly, such as inside-the-oven or window cleaning

Each page should say what's included, what isn't, how long it takes and roughly what it costs.

## Put prices on the site

Cleaning is one of the trades where owners most often hide prices, and one where customers most want them. A starting price or a range by home size answers the question people are searching. It also filters out people who were never going to book.

If you can't publish exact prices, publish what changes the price: square footage, number of bathrooms, pets, condition, frequency.

## Town pages, done properly

If you clean in five towns, a page for each, with something real on it: which neighborhoods you cover, typical home types, how scheduling works there, and reviews from customers in that town. Pages that only swap the town name tend to get ignored by Google.

## Reviews that say something

Ask every recurring customer for a review after their third or fourth visit, once they know you. A review that mentions the service and the town ("great biweekly cleaning in Viera, same cleaner every time") helps both the map pack and AI answers more than "5 stars, great!"

Reply to every review, briefly and personally.

## Trust signals on every page

Say whether you're insured and bonded, whether staff are background checked, whether you bring your own supplies, and what happens if something is missed. Put it near the booking button, not buried in an FAQ.

## Track bookings, not just visits

Count calls, form submissions and online bookings as events in GA4. The [three events every local business should track](/notes/three-events-local-business) are the place to start.

## Next step

Search "house cleaning [your town]" on your phone. If you're not in the map pack, start with the profile. If you are, check whether you have a page for each service above. Our [house cleaning SEO note](/notes/house-cleaning-seo) has more on the residential side.
