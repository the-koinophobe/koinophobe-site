Two parts of your Google Business Profile look similar and do different jobs: categories and services. Mixing them up is one of the most common setup mistakes we see on contractor profiles.

## Categories: what you are

Categories tell Google what kind of business you are. You get **one primary category** and **up to nine additional ones**. The primary carries the most weight in deciding which searches you show up for.

For a roofer, the primary is roofing contractor. For a plumber, plumber. Our [categories by trade list](/notes/gbp-categories-by-trade) has suggestions, and [secondary categories](/notes/gbp-secondary-categories) covers when to add more.

## Services: what you do

Services are the specific jobs you do, listed under your categories. Google suggests some for each category, and you can add your own.

For a roofer, services might be roof repair, roof replacement, roof inspection, storm damage repair, gutter installation. Each can have a short description.

## How they work together

Think of categories as the shelf and services as what's on it. A plumbing category with services listed for water heater installation, drain cleaning and leak repair tells Google and customers exactly what you handle.

A service listed without a matching category, or a category with no services, leaves gaps.

## Common mistakes

**Using services instead of categories.** Listing "HVAC repair" as a service won't make you an HVAC company in Google's eyes. If HVAC is a real part of your business, it needs a category.

**Using categories for every service.** Adding nine categories to cover every job dilutes the profile. Most specific jobs belong in services.

**Stuffing service names.** "Best Cheap Emergency Roof Repair Melbourne FL 24/7" isn't a service name. Keep names short and plain: "Emergency roof repair."

**Listing services you don't do.** Calls you can't take do nothing for you, and they can lead to bad reviews.

**Forgetting descriptions.** A short description per service, in customers' words, helps people decide to call.

## Match your website

Each main service on your profile should have a page on your site. The profile tells Google what you do; the page proves it with details, photos and reviews. If a service has no page, either build one or ask whether it belongs on the profile.

## Keep it current

Review services twice a year. Remove what you've stopped doing, add what you've started, and update descriptions with what customers actually ask about.
