**The short answer:** a monthly SEO report should fit on one page. Calls, forms and bookings from Google; Search Console clicks and impressions against the same month last year; the pages added or fixed, with links; and three to five things for next month. That's it.

If you have to scroll through 30 slides of charts to find out whether the work brought in jobs, the report is protecting the agency. It isn't informing you.

## What goes on the page

**Calls.** How many calls came from the website and your Business Profile this month, against the same month last year. That needs call tracking and a phone tap event in GA4. Without it, nobody can tell you whether SEO is paying for itself; that's the whole argument of [rankings without tracking](/notes/rankings-without-tracking).

![A printed report page with bar charts and pie charts on it](/notes/photos/monthly-seo-report-contractors-1.webp "Photo: Cht Gsml on Unsplash")

**Forms and bookings.** The count, plus one line confirming the form was tested and the email arrived. That line is there because of a mistake. In July 2026 I found a client's form submissions had been dropping since March. The form's email connection needed re-authenticating after an email address change. Four months, and nobody noticed, me included. I test form delivery on every site monthly now.

**Search Console clicks and impressions, against last year.** Use the Compare tab in the Performance report and line the month up with the same month a year earlier. Contracting is seasonal, so last month is the wrong comparison most of the year. On a hurricane shutter site I work on, 37 of 51 clicks in one 28-day window came from a single seasonal hurricane history article. Compare that month with the one before it and you'd draw the wrong conclusion. If you aren't sure where these numbers live, [Search Console for contractors](/notes/google-search-console-for-contractors) shows each report.

**Pages added or fixed.** A plain list with links. "Built a flat roof repair page from the client's job photos and linked it from the main services page" is a line you can check. "Ongoing optimization" isn't.

**Next month.** Three to five items, specific enough that you can ask about them on the next call.

Four numbers and two short lists. If you want a blank version to fill in yourself, here's [my one-page template](/notes/monthly-seo-report-template). To see where calls and forms show up in Google Analytics, [the five GA4 reports worth opening](/notes/ga4-reports-for-contractors) covers it.

## Reading a number honestly

Here's a roofing site I work on in Florida. These are its Search Console numbers for October 2025 to August 2026 against the eleven months before:

| Measure | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Average position | 47.9 | 14.9 |
| Impressions | 5,410 | 139,639 |
| Clicks | 34 | 266 |
| Click-through rate | 0.63% | 0.19% |

Click-through rate fell by more than two thirds. Put that number in red and stop there, and you'd think something broke.

It didn't. Impressions grew 25.8 times. Of the 1,099 searches the site now shows for, 893 didn't show it at all the year before, and a lot of those new appearances were on page two, where hardly anyone clicks. So the share of people clicking went down while the number of people clicking went from 34 to 266. Moving those page-two searches up is where the next clicks come from. The work behind it is on my [roofing SEO page](/roofing-seo).

## What to ignore

**Ranking-only reports.** A table of 50 keywords and their positions tells you very little by itself. Across three Florida sites I work on, rankings improved on all three in one 28-day window without more clicks; 307 searches sat in the top ten with zero clicks. I wrote that up in [zero-click rankings and title tags](/notes/zero-click-rankings-title-tags). Rankings are good for finding problems. Calls and forms are what you're paying for.

**Total impressions with no breakdown.** My own site is the example here. In the three months to November 2026 it had 5,943 impressions in Search Console. 4,015 of them, about two thirds, went to one page explaining "koinophobe", the word my business is named after. Those people want a definition. None of them will ever hire me. A report that celebrated that chart would be lying with a true number.

**Third-party scores.** Domain authority, "visibility scores" and site health percentages come from SEO tools, and Google doesn't use them. They can move without your phone ringing more.

**Bounce rate on its own.** Someone who lands on your page, taps the phone number and leaves is a good visit.
