## Start with the Business Profile

For a shop with a counter people walk up to, the Google Business Profile is the first thing a local searcher sees. Keep the address visible, choose the most specific primary category that fits, and fill in hours, holiday hours, photos and products. Use your real business name with no keywords added. [Choosing categories](/notes/google-business-profile-categories) covers how to pick the primary one, and [auto repair shop SEO](/notes/auto-repair-shop-seo) shows the setup for another walk-in business.

If you're a contractor or service business that goes to the customer, set it up as a service-area business instead. Remove the address, then add up to 20 service areas by city or ZIP code, within about 2 hours of driving. ## Near-me searches come from relevance and distance

Nobody can move your shop closer to the searcher. What you control is how clearly your profile and site say what you sell. Over The Table Top shows up for 398 near-me searches, and the ones in the table above describe what the shop is and what it stocks. Your profile and pages should say what you are and what you sell in the words people search with.

Across five sites I work on, from April 2025 to August 2026, there were 1,250 near-me queries. 780 landed on page one, 191 in the top three, and they brought 343 clicks. The breakdown is in [what 1,250 near-me searches showed](/notes/near-me-searches-local-seo-data).

## Product schema if you sell inventory

If you sell physical goods, Product markup tells Google the name, price and availability of each item. Over The Table Top has product schema on its inventory. The work only pays off if it stays accurate, so whoever updates stock should update the site in the same step. When an item sells out, mark it out of stock or take the listing down.

## A page for each thing you do

Over The Table Top also does custom woodwork. That page was nearly deleted. It now pulls its own traffic.

Most small businesses have something like that: a repair service, a class, a rental, a custom order. If people would search for it on its own, give it its own page with photos, prices or a quote form, and a short answer to the questions you hear at the counter.

## Mention the towns you serve, honestly

Name the Charles County towns your customers come from on your contact page and in the text where it's true. Skip the stack of copy-paste pages for each town. Google's doorway abuse policy treats near-identical town pages as spam.

## Then work on clicks

Rankings that climb without clicks usually point at the snippet: the title tag and meta description that show in results, and whether the page does what the searcher came to do.
