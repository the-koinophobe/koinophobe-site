GA4 can tell you that 40 calls came from Google last month. It can't tell you how many became jobs, or what those jobs were worth. That last step happens in your office, and most businesses don't record it anywhere.

A simple sheet fixes that.

## The columns

One row per lead:

| Date | Name | Source | Service | Town | Quoted? | Booked? | Job value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Source:** Google search, Google Maps (Business Profile), referral, repeat customer, ads, other
- **Service:** what they wanted, in your own categories
- **Town:** where the job is
- **Quoted / Booked:** yes or no
- **Job value:** the invoice total once it's done

That's it. Resist the urge to add more columns until these are filled in every time.

## Getting the source right

The hardest column is source. Use three things together:

1. **Ask on the call.** "How did you find us?" Most people know whether they searched Google, saw the map, or were referred.
2. **Use your tracking.** Calls to a tracking number on your Business Profile are from the map. Forms can carry a hidden source field. See [call tracking software](/notes/call-tracking-software-contractors) and [form tracking in GA4](/notes/ga4-form-tracking-contractors).
3. **Keep the options short.** Six sources you'll actually pick from beat twenty you won't.

## Five minutes a week

Once a week, whoever answers the phone fills in the rows for new leads and updates booked jobs and values. Once a month, add up:

- Leads by source
- Booked jobs by source
- Revenue by source

## What it tells you

- **Which source brings jobs, not just leads.** Google search leads might book more often than a lead site's, for example.
- **Which services and towns bring the best work**, so you know where to build the next pages.
- **What SEO is worth.** Revenue from Google search and the Business Profile, against what you spend on SEO. That's the number that answers whether it's paying off.

Put the monthly totals next to your other numbers. Our [one-page report template](/notes/monthly-seo-report-template) has room for them.

## When to move to a CRM

When several people handle leads, when follow-ups slip, or when you want automatic reminders, a CRM earns its cost. Start with the sheet anyway: it teaches you which fields matter before you pay for software.

## Next step

Make the sheet today and fill in last week's leads from memory and your call log. In a month, you'll know more about where your work comes from than most of your competitors do. For counting the calls in the first place, see [call tracking for contractors](/notes/call-tracking-for-contractors).
