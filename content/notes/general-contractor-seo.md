**The short answer:** homeowners don't search for a job title; they search for the project. "Home addition contractor", "basement finishing", "kitchen remodel" plus the town. So a general contractor's site needs a page for each kind of project you want more of, a portfolio organized the same way, and a Business Profile category that matches your main work. "General contractor near me" is a vague search with a vague audience.

## Why "general contractor" is a weak target

People who search "general contractor" are a mixed bag: homeowners, property managers, people looking for jobs, subcontractors looking for work, students. People who search "home addition contractor [town]" want exactly one thing, and it's what you sell.

Rank for the projects and the job title takes care of itself.

## The project pages

Pick the projects you want more of, not everything you've ever done. For each one, a page with:

- What the project involves, start to finish, including design, permits and inspections.
- A realistic timeline.
- What drives the cost, with ranges from your own jobs if you can.
- Three to six real projects with photos, the town, and a line or two on what was tricky.
- Who does the work: your crew, your subs, who manages the site.

Typical pages: home additions, basement finishing, kitchen remodels, bathroom remodels, whole-home renovations, decks and porches, garage conversions, ADUs where they're allowed. If kitchens and baths are your main line, my [home remodeling SEO](/home-remodeling-seo) page shows how I set that up, and [bathroom remodel SEO](/notes/bathroom-remodel-seo) goes deeper on one project.

## The portfolio

Organize it by project type and link each project to its service page, and each service page back to its projects. A project write-up with photos, the scope and the town is useful twice: as proof on the service page, and as a page that can rank for "[project] in [town]".

## Permits, license and insurance

Homeowners worry about unpermitted work and uninsured crews. A page explaining that you pull permits, schedule inspections and carry insurance, with your license number in the footer, answers that before they ask.

## The Business Profile

Your primary category should match the work most of your money comes from. If that's remodeling, **Remodeler** or a specific remodeler category often fits searches better than **General contractor**; if you build homes, **Home builder**. Add General contractor as a secondary. Check what the top three map results use for your main search before you decide. [Business Profile categories by trade](/notes/gbp-categories-by-trade) has the picks for the main trades.

Post project photos as jobs finish and ask every client for a review that names the project.

## Long sales cycles need tracking

Big projects take weeks or months from first call to signed contract. Track calls and form submissions by page, and keep a simple sheet of which leads became jobs and what they were worth. [Lead tracking sheet](/notes/lead-tracking-sheet) shows a simple version.

## Where to start

Write down the two or three project types you want more of next year. Check whether each has its own page with real projects on it. If not, build those first.
