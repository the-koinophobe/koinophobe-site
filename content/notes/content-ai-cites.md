Most AI SEO advice about content is a vibe: "write authoritative content," "be helpful." I wanted something more specific, so I looked at what the cited pages in [my September 2026 study](/notes/ai-cited-vs-ranked-page) had in common, against pages that ranked on Google for the same question and were never cited.

Here's the shape, and a template you can use for any business.

## What the cited pages looked like

**Article-shaped.** 41.6% of cited pages carried Article or BlogPosting markup. None of the pages that ranked without being cited did. Within companies' own sites, assistants reached for blog posts and guides, while the product and pricing pages ranked and got passed over.

**Longer, a bit.** Median 2,487 words for cited pages against 2,076 for the comparison group. That's descriptive, with no interval computed, so treat it as a hint. Length follows from covering a question fully, not the other way round.

**More specific.** Cited pages named a median of two vendors against one. They compared and qualified instead of describing one product in isolation.

**Mostly vendor-written.** 71.5% of cited pages were vendor-owned. Assistants didn't shut out companies writing about their own field. They cited the ones that wrote useful articles.

## A template that matches

This is the structure I use for client pages meant to be cited. It works for a SaaS comparison, a dentist's explainer or a contractor's cost guide.

**1. A title that is the question, or answers it.** "How much does a metal roof cost in Florida in 2026?" or "Invoice software for agencies: five options compared."

**2. A direct answer in the first two or three sentences.** The number, the recommendation, the yes or no, with the main condition. If a reader stops here, they got their answer. If a system lifts one passage, this is it.

**3. Headings phrased as the follow-up questions.** "What makes it more expensive?" "How long does it last?" "Does insurance cover it?" Google's AI features run related searches across subtopics, and each heading is a chance to match one. [More on that](/notes/google-ai-mode-seo).

**4. Facts with dates and sources.** "Prices checked October 2026." "Florida Building Code requires..." "According to the manufacturer's spec sheet..." Dated, sourced facts are easier to trust and to quote.

**5. Your own experience.** What you've seen on real jobs or with real customers. This is the part a competitor's AI-written page can't copy, and it's what makes a page worth citing over the other forty.

**6. Where the alternative wins.** A page that only praises your option reads as an ad. Name the cases where someone should pick something else.

**7. A named author with a bio.** And Article or BlogPosting markup with author, datePublished and dateModified. [Schema for AI search](/notes/schema-markup-for-ai-search) covers the markup.

**8. Links to related pages.** Each guide links to the service it supports and to neighboring questions. Internal links help crawlers find the cluster.

## What to write about first

Start with the questions where assistants already name competitors and not you. Ask ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google your customers' questions, note who's cited, and write the better page. My [tracking method](/notes/track-ai-search-visibility) makes that list.

Good first pages for most businesses:

- **Cost pages.** "How much does X cost" is asked constantly and rarely answered honestly.
- **Comparisons.** You versus the main alternative, or two options customers weigh. Head-to-head questions drew on the fewest sources in my study, so each cited page gets a bigger share. [Comparison pages](/notes/saas-comparison-pages).
- **"Is it worth it" pages.** Decisions with trade-offs, answered plainly.

## Mistakes I see

- **Answer buried in paragraph six.** Move it to the top.
- **Prices in images, tables as screenshots.** Google advises keeping important content in text. So do I.
- **Undated pages.** Assistants repeat stale facts. Date yours and update them.
- **AI-written filler.** Forty pages that say the same thing as every other site give an assistant no reason to pick yours.
