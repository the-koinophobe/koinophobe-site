Contractors keep asking whether Google's AI Overviews are taking their calls. The honest answer is that it depends on the kind of search, and most of the searches that pay a contractor still look the way they did before.

## Two kinds of home service searches

**Hiring searches.** "Roofer near me," "emergency plumber Palm Bay," "AC repair Melbourne." The person wants a company, now. Google usually answers these with the map pack and local results. That's where the calls come from, and it's still run by your Business Profile, reviews and local pages.

**Question searches.** "How much does a new roof cost," "why is my water heater making noise," "do I need a permit to replace a fence." The person wants to understand something first. These are the searches where AI Overviews appear most, summarizing an answer at the top of the page.

So AI Overviews mostly sit in front of your informational content, not your money pages.

## What that does to your traffic

If you've published cost guides and how-to articles, some of their clicks may drop, because part of the answer now appears on the results page. We've written about [what to do when AI Overviews cut traffic](/notes/ai-overviews-traffic-drop).

Calls are a different story. Someone who learns that a roof replacement costs a certain range still has to hire a roofer. The question for your business is whether your company is the one named, linked or remembered when they get to that step.

## What gets cited in a home service AI Overview

AI Overviews link to sources, usually a handful of pages. The pages that get picked tend to share a few traits:

1. **The answer is near the top.** A clear first paragraph that answers the question in plain words, before the background.
2. **The page is specific.** "Roof replacement cost in Brevard County" beats "Roofing costs explained."
3. **It's easy to read for a machine.** Real text in the page, headings that match the questions, and no content hidden behind scripts. Run the [free AI crawler check](/ai-search-optimization#check) to be sure assistants can read you.
4. **The site already ranks.** In our [citation study](/notes/ai-cited-vs-ranked-page), most companies AI answers named also ranked on Google. Ordinary SEO is still the way in.

## What we'd change on a contractor site

- **Turn your FAQ into pages.** Each common question you answer on the phone deserves its own short page with the answer in the first two sentences.
- **Add numbers you can stand behind.** Price ranges from your own jobs, typical timelines, what's included. Specifics get quoted; vague pages don't.
- **Keep the Business Profile and the site saying the same thing.** Services, towns and hours that match everywhere make it easier to name you with confidence.
- **Mark up the basics.** LocalBusiness or a trade-specific type, your services and your FAQ. Our [schema guide for AI search](/notes/schema-markup-for-ai-search) shows what to add.

## How to tell if it's happening to you

In Search Console, compare clicks and impressions for your informational pages year on year. If impressions hold or rise while clicks fall, AI answers are likely taking some of those clicks. If your calls are flat or up at the same time, the money pages are doing their job.
