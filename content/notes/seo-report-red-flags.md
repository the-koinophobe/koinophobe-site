Most agencies aren't trying to fool anyone. But reports are easy to fill with numbers that look good and say little. Here are nine things to watch for, and the question that gets to the truth.

## 1. No calls or forms anywhere

The single most important number for a contractor is leads from Google. If the report doesn't have it, it can't tell you whether SEO is working.

**Ask:** "How many calls and forms came from organic search last month, compared with the same month last year?"

## 2. Rankings without clicks

A screenshot of position 3 for a search nobody makes is easy to produce. Rankings matter only if they bring clicks, and clicks matter only if they bring calls. We explain why in [rankings without tracking](/notes/rankings-without-tracking).

**Ask:** "How many clicks did these keywords bring?"

## 3. "We rank for 2,000 keywords"

Most of those are long-tail searches in positions nobody sees. A short list of searches that bring jobs, with real positions, is worth more.

**Ask:** "Which ten searches matter most for my business, and where are we for each?"

## 4. Month-over-month comparisons only

Home service demand is seasonal. Comparing August to July can make a flat month look like growth, or a normal winter look like a crash.

**Ask:** "How does this compare with the same month last year?"

## 5. Scores from paid tools instead of Google data

Domain authority and visibility scores are estimates made by tool companies. They're not what Google uses, and they don't show leads.

**Ask:** "What do Search Console and GA4 say?"

## 6. Vague work

"Ongoing optimization," "technical improvements," "link building in progress." If you can't open a page or see a change, you can't check the work.

**Ask:** "Which pages were published or changed, and can I see them?"

## 7. The same next steps every month

If next month's plan reads like last month's, the work isn't being driven by results.

**Ask:** "What did last month's work change, and what are we doing differently because of it?"

## 8. Traffic up, no breakdown

Traffic can rise from blog posts read by people outside your service area, or from bots. Without knowing which pages grew, the number means little.

**Ask:** "Which pages did the extra traffic go to, and did any of it call?"

## 9. Accounts you don't own

If GA4, Search Console or your Business Profile are in the agency's name, the history leaves when they do.

**Ask:** "Can you add me as owner on all three accounts?"

## What a good report looks like

One page: leads, Business Profile actions, clicks, a short list of positions, the work done, and next month's plan, with a column for the same month last year.
