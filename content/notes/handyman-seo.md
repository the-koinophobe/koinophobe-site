**The short answer:** don't build one "handyman services" page with forty bullet points. Build a page for each job people search by name (TV mounting, drywall patching, door repair, furniture assembly, caulking, light fixtures), show your rate or minimum charge, and get a review out of every job. A handyman's best customer is the one who calls you again for the next ten small things.

## How people search for a handyman

Some search "handyman near me". Many more search the job: "tv wall mounting", "fix hole in drywall", "replace door knob", "assemble ikea dresser". They don't think of it as handyman work; they think of it as the thing that's been bugging them for a month.

So the site has to meet them at the job. A single services page that lists everything ranks for nothing in particular.

## Which jobs get their own page

Pick the jobs that come up most often and pay well for the time, then give each a page:

- **TV mounting.** One of the most searched. Say which wall types you mount on, whether you hide cables, and the price for a standard mount.
- **Drywall repair.** Holes, cracks, water stains. Photos of patches before and after paint are your best sales tool.
- **Door repair and installation.** Sticking doors, new hardware, interior and exterior doors.
- **Furniture and flat-pack assembly.**
- **Light fixtures, fans and smart devices,** within what your state lets you do without an electrician.
- **Small carpentry, trim and shelving.**
- **Caulking, grout and small bathroom fixes.**

Each page: what's included, how long it usually takes, the price or how it's priced, a few photos of real jobs, and the phone number at the top. Then one "everything else" page that lists the smaller stuff and says "if it's not here, ask".

## Prices and the minimum charge

Most handymen have a minimum, a trip charge or an hourly rate with a one-hour minimum. Put it on the site. People shopping for a handyman are usually comparing a few, and being clear up front filters out the calls that end at the price.

A good trick: list a "small jobs list" price. Two hours, as many small fixes as fit. Customers save up their list, and you get a full visit instead of a 20-minute one.

## License limits

In many states a handyman can only take jobs under a dollar limit, or can't do certain work like electrical or plumbing without a license. Check your state's rules and say plainly on the site what you take on and what you refer out. It saves you from awkward calls and it reads as honest, which is what people want from someone they're letting into the house.

## The Business Profile

Primary category: **Handyman**. Add secondary categories only for trades you do regularly and legally. [Business Profile categories by trade](/notes/gbp-categories-by-trade) has the picks for the main home service trades.

List each job in the Services section, add photos after every job, and keep the hours true. If you work from home and don't serve customers there, hide the address and set your service areas.

## Reviews and repeat customers

Handyman work is repeat work. Someone who liked how you mounted their TV will call you for the fence gate, the leaky faucet and the closet shelves. So:

- Ask for a review the day of the job, with a direct link by text. [How to ask for Google reviews](/notes/how-to-ask-for-google-reviews) has the wording.
- Leave a card or a magnet, and send a short text a few months later.
- Ask reviewers to mention the job. "Mounted our TV and hid the cables" helps you rank for TV mounting more than "great service".
