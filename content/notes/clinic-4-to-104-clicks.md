A local myofascial release clinic in Florida got 4 clicks from Google in September 2025. In April 2026 it got 104. It has held between about 83 and 104 a month for most of the year since: 854 clicks over twelve months, from a site that started with almost none. Here is the work behind those numbers, in the order it happened.

These figures come from the clinic's own Search Console export, monthly totals. An earlier version of this note said "158 to 501 clicks in six months". No equal window in the data shows that, so it has been corrected.

## Step one: move the site without losing anything

The clinic was moving from an old site to a new one. Every old address was pointed at its new home with a permanent redirect, so nothing Google already trusted was thrown away. Moves like this are where small sites quietly lose years of standing, and it's the step most people skip.

## Step two: rebuild the site

The new site was built properly from the ground up: pages Google can find and read, fast on a phone, one clear heading per page.

## Step three: a page for every treatment

The biggest change was replacing one generic services page with a page for each treatment, plus supporting pages that answer what patients actually ask before they book: what the treatment involves, what it helps with, what a first visit looks like. Patients don't search in clinical terms. They search for their pain, their town, and "near me".

![A physical therapist measuring the flexibility of a patient's leg during a treatment session](/notes/photos/clinic-158-to-501-1.webp "Photo: Navy Medicine on Unsplash")

## Step four: the basics on every page

Titles, descriptions and the words patients use, on every page. Each title was written to earn the click, not just label the page. Today 415 different "near me" searches show this clinic, and phones carry most of the clicks at an 11.3% click-through rate.

## Step five: tracking

Calls, form sends and booking requests are counted in the clinic's own Google Analytics, so they can check what the site does without taking our word for it.

## What the numbers did, including the dip

Monthly clicks went from 4 to 104, and impressions from 177 to 1,967 a month. Then, over the summer of 2026, the average position slid: about 14 in May, about 31 in August. It came back to around 20 by October. We don't know why, and we're not going to guess. The lesson is the useful part: your standing on Google is never finished. Other people keep changing the pages around yours.

## What this means for your business

The playbook isn't secret. Move carefully, build pages around what customers ask rather than your internal service menu, write titles that earn the click, and measure what comes in.
