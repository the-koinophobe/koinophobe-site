**The short answer:** appliance repair searches come in three shapes: the appliance ("dryer repair"), the brand ("Bosch dishwasher repair"), and the town ("appliance repair [town]"). Your site needs a clear page for each one you actually serve, your Business Profile needs to match how you really work, and the diagnostic fee belongs on the page. Most of the calls get lost between the search and the phone, not in some advanced SEO problem.

In November 2026 I audited an appliance repair company that looked like it was doing everything right. Strong rating, plenty of reviews, a page for each appliance, each brand and about 60 towns. It wasn't on page one for its own city plus "appliance repair". Here's what was in the way, because the same problems show up on most appliance repair sites I look at.

## What was going wrong

**Google got almost no labels from the homepage.** The homepage title was just the business name. It didn't say "appliance repair" or the city. There was no main heading, and none of the pages I checked had a search description or business markup. Google can still work out what the site is about, but it's being asked to guess.

**Three town pages had broken addresses.** A hidden special character had replaced the letters "fi" in their web addresses, almost certainly pasted in from a PDF. Type the normal address and you got an error page. An old copy of the homepage was also still live and listed for Google, competing with the real one.

**A competitor's ad showed on the business's own name.** Someone searching for the company by name, someone who already wanted them, saw a rival's sponsored listing with more reviews right next to it.

**One of the top reviews was for a different kind of business.** It praised food and coffee. That usually means the listing once belonged to someone else, or a review landed in the wrong place. Customers find it confusing, and a profile that changed businesses is a suspension risk.

**Small trust leaks everywhere.** The site's review badge showed a lower count than Google. Several town names were misspelled. A menu item went to the wrong page. The brand logos linked to a website theme's demo site.

None of this needs a big budget. Most of it is a day or two of careful cleanup.

## How I'd set up an appliance repair site

**One page per appliance you fix.** Refrigerator, washer, dryer, dishwasher, oven and range, microwave, ice maker. Each one lists the common problems in the words people use ("fridge not cooling", "washer won't drain"), what a visit involves, and the brands you service for that appliance.

**Brand pages, only for brands you really service.** Brand searches are specific and people making them are ready to book. Say which models you handle and whether you work on high-end brands. If you don't touch a brand, leave it out; a page that brings calls you turn away costs you twice.

**Town pages that say something about the town.** Sixty town pages with the town name swapped are sixty pages Google learns to ignore. Fewer, better ones beat them: the towns you actually drive to, with jobs you've done there, how quickly you usually get out, and anything local that matters, like older homes with older appliances.

**The fee, the warranty and the license, in plain text.** Diagnostic fee, whether it's credited toward the repair, the warranty on parts and labor, and your license and insurance. These are the questions people call to ask, and answering them on the page makes the call a booking instead of a price check.

## The Business Profile

Use **Appliance repair service** as the primary category. If you go to customers' homes and don't serve people at your address, Google's rules say to hide the address and list your service areas instead; it also keeps a home address private. Post photos from real jobs, and ask every customer for a review with a short link sent by text the same day. [How to ask for Google reviews](/notes/how-to-ask-for-google-reviews) covers the wording.

If your profile has history you don't recognize, like old reviews for another business, sort that out before building on it. [Suspended profiles](/notes/google-business-profile-suspended) covers what triggers a review.

## Your own name

Search your business name. If a competitor's ad sits next to your listing, you have two fixes: make your listing the obvious choice (photos, full services, recent reviews) and consider a small ad on your own name. It's usually cheap because nobody else is as relevant for that search. [Ranking for your business name](/notes/ranking-for-your-business-name) covers the rest.

## Track the calls

Appliance repair runs on the phone. Without call tracking, you can't tell whether the brand pages or the town pages bring work. [Call tracking for contractors](/notes/call-tracking-for-contractors) shows the free setup.
