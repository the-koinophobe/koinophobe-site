Most of the sites I work on are roofing, pool deck, lawn and shutter companies. Tree work isn't one of them. Tree service searches run on the same rules, and they share a lot with roofing, because both trades get flooded with calls after a storm. So this is the setup I'd build for a tree company, and the results I mention come from the trades I do work in.

## Two kinds of demand

Tree work comes in two shapes.

Storm calls are sudden. A limb is on the roof, a tree is across the driveway, a trunk split overnight. The person searches `emergency tree removal` or `tree service near me` on a phone, looks at the map, and calls the first company that looks available. These calls bunch up in the days after a storm and disappear when the weather clears.

Planned work is slower. A homeowner wants a dead oak taken down before it falls, a hedge line trimmed back from the house, or old stumps ground out before new sod goes in. They search `tree removal [town]` or `stump grinding [town]`, look at a few sites, check photos and reviews, and ask for quotes.

Your Business Profile and a tap-to-call button carry the storm calls. Service pages carry the planned work.

## Service pages by job

Give each job its own page, because each is its own search:

![A tree service crew using heavy machinery on a tree removal job](/notes/photos/tree-service-seo-1.webp "Photo: Mayer Tree Service on Unsplash")

- **Tree removal.** Say how you handle trees near houses, fences and power lines, whether you haul everything away, and whether stump grinding is included or extra.
- **Tree trimming.** Crown thinning, raising, clearing branches off the roof, and how often a tree should be trimmed.
- **Stump grinding.** How deep you grind, what happens to the chips, and whether you do single stumps or only jobs with removal.
- **Emergency storm cleanup.** What counts as an emergency, the towns you cover after hours, how fast you respond, and a line on working with insurance if you do.

Each page needs a title tag naming the job and your main town, a tap-to-call button near the top, a quote form with a photo upload, and the questions customers ask before they book. A photo of the tree lets you quote a lot of jobs before you drive out.

## Show insurance and credentials if they're real

Tree work damages property when it goes wrong, and homeowners know it. If you carry liability insurance and workers' compensation, say so on every service page and on the quote page. Offering a certificate of insurance on request is worth a line too.

Credentials need more care. Only say "ISA Certified Arborist" if someone on your crew holds that credential from the International Society of Arboriculture right now. ISA has a public directory where homeowners can look up credentialed arborists. If you have one, name the person and say what they do on your jobs. If you don't, describe your experience in plain terms and leave the word "certified" off the site.

## Before and after photos

Tree work is easy to photograph. Take one photo from the same spot before you start and one when the yard is cleaned up. Put each pair on the matching service page and town page with a few lines underneath: the job, the town, the size of the tree, how long it took. File names like `tree-removal-[town]-before.webp` give Google something to match. My note on [before and after photos](/notes/before-after-gallery-seo) has the full routine, and the same photos belong on your Google Business Profile as jobs finish.

## Time pages to the weather

Storm pages have to be live before the storm. A new page can take weeks or months to be indexed and settle into a position, so write the emergency storm cleanup page well ahead of your storm season. On the Atlantic and Gulf coasts that's hurricane season, June 1 to November 30, with the peak around September 10. Inland, it depends on your region's wind, ice and thunderstorm patterns.

Seasonal articles can pull more weight than service pages in the right window. A hurricane shutter company I work with got 37 of its 51 clicks in one 28-day window from a single seasonal article about hurricane history. For a tree company, the matching article answers what homeowners ask before and after a storm. My [storm damage roofing pages](/notes/storm-damage-roofing-pages) note covers the page types for roofers, and the planning carries over.

Planned trimming has its own timing. Many homeowners book it for late fall and winter, when trees are dormant and branches are easier to see. Open Search Console's Performance report, compare the same months last year, and publish or refresh each page a couple of months before its searches rise. The [seasonal content calendar](/notes/seasonal-content-calendar-home-services) lays out how I plan that.

## Town pages and the profile

A page for each town you want work in can help with `tree service [town]` searches. Each needs something true about the town: the tree species you see most, storm damage you've cleared there, how fast you get out. Near-identical pages with the town name swapped count as doorway spam under Google's policies. [One real page per town](/notes/roofing-service-area-pages) explains how to write them.

On the Business Profile, set up to 20 service areas by city or ZIP, use your real business name with no keywords added, and set special hours if you run extended storm hours. [Service area businesses on Google](/notes/service-area-business-google-business-profile) covers how far those areas reach in practice.
