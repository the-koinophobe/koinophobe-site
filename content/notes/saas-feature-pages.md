Most SaaS sites I look at have a homepage, a pricing page, a blog and not much in between. The homepage tries to speak to everyone. The blog covers broad topics. Nothing answers the searches a buyer makes while they're deciding: "[category] for agencies," "[tool] Slack integration," "how to automate [task]."

Those in-between pages are where a lot of SaaS search traffic converts. Here's what I'd build and what goes on each.

## Use case pages

A use case page is written for one kind of customer with one job to do. "Client reporting for marketing agencies" is a use case. "Powerful analytics" is a slogan.

Each one should cover:

- **The job, in the customer's words.** Use the phrases from sales calls and support tickets.
- **How they do it today** and what goes wrong.
- **How your product handles it,** step by step, with screenshots of the real interface.
- **Proof from that kind of customer:** a quote, a short case study, a number if you have permission to share it.
- **What it doesn't do,** if there's a common expectation you don't meet.

Five to fifteen good use case pages beat fifty thin ones. If two pages would say the same thing with a different job title swapped in, they're one page.

## Feature pages

Build a page for a feature when people search for it by name, or when it's the reason customers pick you. Explain what it does, show it working, list the plans that include it, and link to the docs.

In [my citation study](/notes/ai-cited-vs-ranked-page), 41.6% of the pages AI assistants cited were marked up as articles, against none of the pages that ranked on Google without being cited. Assistants favored pages that explain over pages that pitch. A feature page that answers "how does this work and when would I use it" in plain prose has a better chance of being quoted than one made of icons and three-word headings. [How to write content AI assistants cite](/notes/content-ai-cites) covers the format.

## Integration pages

People search "[your tool] + [other tool]" when they're checking whether you fit their stack. That's late in the buying process, so these pages convert well when they answer the question.

For each integration:

- What it does in one sentence, and the main reason someone would turn it on.
- What syncs, in which direction, and how often.
- Setup steps, with screenshots.
- Limits and known issues. Buyers find them anyway; better they find them from you.
- A link to the partner's listing of your integration, and ask the partner to link back. [Startup backlinks](/notes/startup-backlinks) covers why partner links are some of the most useful you can get.

## Where templated pages tip into spam

It's tempting to generate a page for every integration, city, job title and industry from one template. Google's spam policies call this scaled content abuse when "many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users." Its doorway abuse examples include "substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy."

A template is fine. Each page still needs something true and specific that the others don't have. If you have 200 integrations, write full pages for the 20 people use most and keep the rest in a filterable directory on one page.

## Structure and linking

- **Hub pages.** A use cases index, a features index, an integrations directory. Each links to every page under it, and each page links back.
- **Cross-links.** Use case pages link to the features they rely on and the integrations that matter for that customer.
- **Rendered on the server.** If these pages are built in a client-side React app, many AI crawlers see nothing. [JavaScript SEO for React sites](/notes/javascript-seo-react) has the test.

Comparison and alternatives pages are their own category; [SaaS comparison pages](/notes/saas-comparison-pages) covers them.

## Measure by page

Track sign-ups and demo requests by landing page. After a few months you'll see which use cases bring buyers, and that tells you where to write next.

## Where to start

List your top five use cases from sales calls and your top ten integrations by usage. Write those fifteen pages before anything else on the blog. [SEO for startups](/notes/seo-for-startups) covers the order of the rest.
