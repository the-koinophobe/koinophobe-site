A new startup domain has no links, and without some, even good pages struggle to rank. The free links worth having are mostly earned, and the fastest ones come from people who already know you.

Here's where I'd look, in the order I'd do it. I made the same list for my own site this month.

## Start with people who already know you

**Integration partners.** If your product connects to other tools, their app directories or marketplaces usually list integrations with a link to your site. These are some of the most relevant links you'll ever get, because the linking site is in your category. Apply as soon as the integration ships.

**Customers.** Some customers publish their stack or a "tools we use" page. Ask the ones who like you. A case study you write together often ends up on their site too.

**Investors, accelerators and programs.** Portfolio pages and program directories link to the companies in them. Check that your listing has the right URL.

**Your founders' own profiles.** Conference speaker pages, podcast guest notes and alumni pages. Small, but real.

## Publish something worth citing

Links come to pages people want to reference. For a startup, two things work well.

**Original data.** Statistics roundups and journalists link to the source behind each number. In September 2026 I ran [a citation study](/notes/ai-cited-vs-ranked-page) across 96 AI searches, and every number in it is something other sites can cite with a link. Your product probably already collects data that would interest your market, anonymized and aggregated.

**A free tool.** Something small that does one job without a sign-up. My [AI crawler check](/ai-search-optimization#check) checks whether ten search and AI crawlers can read a site. Tools like that get added to "free tools" lists, and some of those links are followed.

Then tell people. Find the articles that already cover your topic (search "[your topic] statistics 2026" or "best free [category] tools") and email the authors with one specific thing to add.

## Answer journalist requests

Reporters post requests for expert quotes every day. Answer the ones in your field with something specific, and some will quote and link you.

- **Source of Sources** is free, run by HARO's original founder.
- **Featured**, which now owns the HARO brand, gives free accounts three quotes a month.
- **Qwoted** is free to join, with a delay on free pitches.
- **#journorequest** on X and Bluesky often shows requests before the digests do.

Answer fast, stay on topic, and give the reporter a line they can use as written.

## Directories worth a profile

A few software directories are worth an afternoon: SaaSHub (reported to give a followed link), AlternativeTo, G2 and Capterra (free basic profiles), and Product Hunt for a launch. Product Hunt links are nofollow, so the value there is launch-day traffic and attention.

Nofollow links still help in other ways. They send visitors, and they help search engines and AI assistants recognize your company as a real business. In [my study](/notes/ai-cited-vs-ranked-page), 20.1% of the sources Google's AI Overviews cited were forums and social sites, which almost never pass link credit. Being mentioned there still counts. [Brand mentions and AI search](/notes/brand-mentions-ai-search) covers that side.

## What to skip

Google's spam policies list these as link spam:

- **Buying or selling links.**
- **"Low-quality directory or bookmark site links,"** which describes most of the "submit to 300 directories" packages.
- **"Widely distributed links in the footers or templates of various sites."**
- **Links with optimized anchor text in guest posts or press releases** distributed on other sites.

Link exchanges and private blog networks belong on the same list. Skip them even when a competitor seems to get away with it.

## Where to start

This week: list every integration partner, investor and program you're part of and check whether each one links to you. Then pick one thing worth citing, either data or a tool, and put it on the roadmap. [SEO for startups](/notes/seo-for-startups) covers the pages to build alongside it.
