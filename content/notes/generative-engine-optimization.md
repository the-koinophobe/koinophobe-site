Generative engine optimization, GEO for short, is the name that stuck for getting your business into answers written by AI: ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Google's AI Overviews. You'll also hear AEO (answer engine optimization), AI SEO, and LLM SEO. They point at the same goal, and I compare them in [GEO vs SEO vs AEO](/notes/geo-vs-seo-vs-aeo).

The term comes from a 2023 academic paper titled "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization." It spread fast because business owners noticed customers asking assistants instead of searching. The advice spread faster than the evidence. This note sorts the two.

## What GEO is trying to change

An AI answer has two parts a business can care about:

- **The mention.** Your company is named in the text: "Good options include Acme Plumbing and..."
- **The citation.** One of your pages is linked as a source.

In [my September 2026 study](/notes/ai-cited-vs-ranked-page), those two behaved very differently. Assistants named the same companies Google ranked 34% to 54% of the time, but cited the same pages only 2% to 12% of the time. So GEO has two jobs that need different work: being a company the web recognizes, and having pages assistants choose to quote.

## GEO advice that holds up

**Let the right crawlers in.** If OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot can't fetch your pages, nothing else matters. OpenAI says this directly for ChatGPT search. It's the one GEO step that works by definition, and the most common one I find broken. [How to check](/notes/ai-crawlers-robots-txt).

**Rank on Google for the questions buyers ask.** The company overlap above is the strongest pattern in my data. Google-ranked companies are the pool assistants pick from.

**Publish article-shaped pages.** 41.6% of the pages assistants cited carried Article markup, against none of the pages that ranked and were never cited. The markup is a trace of the page type: guides, comparisons and roundups get cited, product pages mostly don't. [Content that AI cites](/notes/content-ai-cites) goes deeper.

**Get talked about elsewhere.** Google's AI Overviews pulled 20.1% of its citations from forums and social sites in my study, and 14.6% from independent roundups. Being discussed off your own site is part of the job. See [Reddit and AI search](/notes/reddit-ai-search).

## GEO advice my data doesn't support

**"Add structured data to get cited."** Having any structured data at all didn't separate cited pages from pages that ranked and were never cited: 74.3% against 83.3%, inside the noise and pointing the wrong way.

**"Make it server-rendered and fast and you'll be cited."** Server-rendering matters for being read at all, but it didn't separate the groups either, because nearly everything that ranks is already server-rendered.

**"Add an llms.txt file."** Google said in 2025 that no AI system currently uses it. [More here](/notes/llms-txt).

**"We'll get you into ChatGPT in 30 days."** Asked the same question three times, ChatGPT kept 4.5% of its cited pages. Google's AI Overviews kept 62.7%. A channel that moves that much can't be promised.

## How to measure GEO without fooling yourself

Freeze a list of 10 to 20 buyer questions. Ask them across several assistants, in fresh sessions, more than once for the ones that matter. Count how often you're named and how often you're linked. Compare month to month on the same list. That's the method from my study, scaled down, and I wrote it up as [how to track AI search visibility](/notes/track-ai-search-visibility).

If an agency's GEO report is one screenshot per question, it's measuring luck.

## Where GEO fits for a business owner

For most businesses, GEO is SEO with two additions: checking crawler access for AI bots, and measuring the answers directly. The pages that win on Google and the pages that get cited are both built on clear, useful content about real questions. [Is SEO dead?](/notes/is-seo-dead) No. It's the foundation GEO stands on.
