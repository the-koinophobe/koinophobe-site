Johnny Urena, a client in Lawrence, Massachusetts, hired me on Upwork to build a free estimate page. His review afterward: "Straight forward, doesn't waste time. If he can't do something he'll tell you."

A free estimate page looks like the simplest page on a site. It's also the one that decides whether all the SEO work turns into a lead. Here's what I put on every one now.

## Short form, the right fields

Every extra field costs you people. Name, phone, email, town, what you need, and an optional photo upload. That's it. The photo matters more than it looks. A roofer, a tint shop or a paving company can quote half the job from one picture, and the person who took the time to upload one is serious.

Ask for the town. People will give you the full address after you've called them back.

## Say where you work

List the towns you serve on the page in plain words. If you're in Lawrence, that's probably Methuen, Andover, Haverhill, Lowell and North Andover. People check before they fill out the form, and so does Google.

## Say when they'll hear back

"We'll call you within one business day" does more than any trust badge. Then keep the promise.

## Make the phone number tappable

A lot of people land on the estimate page on a phone and decide to call instead. The number should be a real tap-to-call link, near the top, and it should be tracked.

## Track the submission itself

The form should fire an event when it's actually submitted. Tracking a visit to a thank-you URL counts bots and people who refresh. Mark that event as a key event in Google Analytics so it shows up in every report, next to the search that brought the person in.

## Check the form still sends

This is the one nobody does. Forms stop sending all the time: an email password changes, a caching plugin blocks the script, a mail setting expires. In July I found form submissions on a client's site had been dropping since March. It was an email setting. Four months, and nobody caught it, me included. I test form delivery on every site I manage every month now.

## Spam without the friction

Use a hidden honeypot field and server-side checks before you reach for a puzzle captcha. Every "click all the traffic lights" screen loses you a few real people.

## The page, in one list

- Five fields plus an optional photo
- The towns you serve, in plain words
- When they'll hear back
- A tap-to-call number, tracked
- A submission event marked as a key event
- A monthly test that the form still delivers
