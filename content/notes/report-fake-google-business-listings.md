In some trades, the map pack is crowded with listings that shouldn't be there: lead generators posing as local companies, the same business listed three times under different names, and profile names stuffed with keywords like "Best Roof Repair Orlando FL." Locksmiths and garage door repair are well known for it. Real companies with honest profiles get pushed down.

You can report them. Done with evidence, it sometimes works. Done as a ranking tactic against competitors you don't like, it wastes your time.

## Fake, or just a competitor?

Report a listing when you can finish this sentence with a fact: "This listing breaks the rules because..."

**Reportable:**

- The business doesn't exist at all, or the address is a house, an empty lot or a mailbox store with no business in it.
- The same company has several listings under different names or tracking numbers.
- The profile name adds services or a city that aren't part of the real business name on its signs, website or state registration.
- The phone number or website on the listing routes to a lead-selling service.

**Not reportable:**

- They rank above you.
- They have more reviews or a better rating.
- They serve the same towns, or hide their address as a service area business. That's allowed. [Service area businesses on Google](/notes/service-area-business-google-business-profile) explains the rules.

## Gather evidence first

Before you report anything, collect:

- Screenshots of the listing, with the date.
- Street View of the address, and a photo if you can drive past.
- The real business name from its website, vehicles or your state's business registry.
- Search results showing the same phone number on several listings, if that's the issue.

Keep it all in one folder. You may need it more than once.

## Use the right tool

Google has three routes, and picking the wrong one slows things down.

**Suggest an edit,** for one wrong detail. On the listing, choose to suggest an edit and correct the field. For a keyword-stuffed name, enter the real business name. This is the fastest route and often the one that works.

**Report the listing,** for one that shouldn't exist. Use the report option on the listing in Google Maps and choose the reason that matches your evidence, such as the place not existing or being a duplicate.

**The [Business Redressal Complaint Form](https://support.google.com/business/contact/business_redressal_complaint),** for fraud. Google says it's for "misleading information or fraudulent activity on Google Maps related to the name, phone number, or URL of a business." It asks for the listing's URL, a screenshot and a description, and accepts a spreadsheet if you're reporting many listings. Fake reviews go through a separate route: report the review itself.

## What to expect

Google doesn't promise removal, and it won't tell you much about what it decided. Edits can show up in days; complaints can take weeks. If a listing comes back, report it again with the same evidence and note that it reappeared.

Keep your own profile clean while you do this. A business that reports keyword-stuffed names while using one itself is easy to spot. Your profile name should be your real name, your category should match your main work ([how to choose](/notes/google-business-profile-categories)), and your reviews should come from real customers asked the same way.

Search your main service in your town and look at every listing in the top ten. Note the ones you can prove are breaking the rules and start with name edits, since those are the quickest.
