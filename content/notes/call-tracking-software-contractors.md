There are two ways to count calls from Google, and they cost very different amounts. Most contractors start with the wrong one: either nothing, or a paid tool they set up once and never open.

Here's how to decide.

## Level one: free tracking

The free setup counts what you can count without changing your phone number:

- **Taps on your phone number on your website**, recorded as an event in GA4 through Google Tag Manager. The [GTM phone click guide](/notes/gtm-phone-click-tracking) walks through it.
- **Calls from your Business Profile**, which Google counts for you under Performance.
- **Form submissions**, as a second event.

For a contractor whose marketing is mostly Google, this answers the main question: are more people calling from search this month than last?

What it can't see: anyone who reads your number and dials it by hand, anyone who calls from a yard sign or truck, and whether a call was answered.

## Level two: call tracking software

Paid call tracking gives each source its own phone number. The website shows one number to visitors from Google, another to visitors from ads, and the Business Profile carries a third. Every call to those numbers is logged with its source, length and outcome.

It earns its cost when:

1. **You spend on more than one channel.** Google Ads, Local Services Ads, Facebook and SEO together. You need to know which made the call.
2. **Many calls are dialled by hand.** Older customers and people on desktop often read the number and type it in. Taps never see them.
3. **You want to know about missed calls.** Software shows calls that rang out. For many contractors, that's the most expensive number they've never seen.
4. **You review calls for quality.** Recordings show how leads are handled, if you have consent to record.

If none of those apply, stay on level one for now.

## Setting it up without hurting local SEO

The worry with tracking numbers is that your name, address and phone number stop matching across the web. Handle it like this:

- **Business Profile:** put the tracking number in the primary phone field and your real number in the additional phone field. Directories that list your real number still match.
- **Website:** use dynamic number insertion, which swaps the number shown based on where the visitor came from. Your real number stays in the page code and schema.
- **Directories and citations:** leave your real number everywhere else. Our [NAP citations guide](/notes/nap-citations-contractors) explains why consistency matters.

## What to look for in a tool

Without naming brands, the features that matter for a contractor:

- **Numbers local to your area code.** Local numbers get answered and called back more readily than toll-free ones.
- **GA4 integration**, so calls show up next to forms in the same reports.
- **Missed call alerts**, by text or email, so someone calls back while the lead is still warm.
- **Simple pricing by number and minutes.** You probably need three to five numbers, not fifty.
- **Recording you can switch off.** Some states require every party's consent to record.

## Read the numbers every month

Tracking is only worth paying for if someone looks. Add calls by source to your [monthly report](/notes/monthly-seo-report-template), next to forms and Business Profile actions, and compare against the same month last year.
